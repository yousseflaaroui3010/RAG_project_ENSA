# Estimating

## The research is brutal and it has not improved

- Reviews of software estimation put the average effort overrun at roughly **30 to 44 percent**. Most find 60 to 80 percent of projects overrun.
- Confidence is worse than the estimate. When a professional gives a minimum and maximum they are 90 percent sure of, reality lands inside it only **60 to 70 percent** of the time.
- Listing more risks was found to **increase** over confidence, not reduce it. A risk workshop can make you more wrong.
- People in technical roles perceive that they are judged as more skilled when they give **low** estimates. The incentive points the wrong way, quietly, always.

Treat the percentages as orders of magnitude. Consultancy figures on this are inflated and the researchers say so. The direction is what matters.

## So never give one number

Three parts, every time.

1. **A range.** "Three to six days", not "four days".
2. **What makes it the bad end.** Name the specific thing. "If the payment provider's sandbox is down again, the top of the range."
3. **What gets cut first.** Decided now, while nobody is under pressure.

A single number is a promise nobody can keep. A range with a named risk and a named cut is a plan.

## When you are asked to commit to the low end

Say what comes off. Not "I will try". Scope, deadline or quality: one of the three gives, and if nobody picks, quality gives by default and silently.

## Padding is not the fix

Doubling a guess produces a bigger guess. What actually helps:

- Split the work until each piece is a day or less. Small pieces estimate better and reveal the unknowns while they are still cheap.
- Say out loud which pieces you have done before and which you have never done. The never-done ones carry the whole risk.
- Compare against how long a similar past task really took, not how long you think it should take.
- If a piece cannot be estimated because you do not understand it yet, that is not an estimate problem. Timebox a look first, then estimate.

## While the work runs

Bad news early, not on the deadline. The journal is what makes that automatic: `Left` as a list of items, blockers with a name, a date and a fallback. See `work-journal`.

A journal that only ever looks fine until the deadline is not being kept.

## Junior against senior

| Moment | Junior | Senior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asked how long | Gives the number they hope for | Range, what blows it up, what gets cut |
| A piece they have never done | Estimates it like the rest | Names it as the unknown carrying the risk |
| Pressed to go lower | Agrees | Names the trade off out loud |
| Running late | Says almost done | Names the remaining items |
| A missed date | Explains afterwards | The record showed it coming two weeks earlier |
